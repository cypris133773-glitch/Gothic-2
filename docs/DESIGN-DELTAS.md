# Where GRIMWARD deliberately differs

Every item here is a decision, not a bug. A reviewer who knows the tradition
this game comes from will notice each one; this file is the answer.

**Movement is smoother.** The original's near-tank controls are a product of
2001 input conventions, not of design intent. Commitment frames stay — you
cannot cancel a swing, and that is the whole combat system — but the friction of
turning around does not.

**There is no stamina bar.** Same as the original. Attack spam is punished by
recovery frames and by enemies who counter the third swing, not by a resource
that empties. A stamina bar would make the fight about a number instead of about
distance.

**Floored hits are legible.** The damage formula is the original's, including
the part where a non-critical hit does a tenth of the raw figure and the result
floors at 5. Against a well-armoured target that reads as "my weapon does
nothing", which is *true* and is meant to be. So a floored hit gets its own
presentation — a clang, sparks, and no hitstop — instead of the meaty impact of
a real one. The maths is untouched; only the feedback is.

**One save slot per character, plus autosaves.** Autosave on region change,
chapter change and quest state change. Manual save anywhere except during combat
and dialogue. Save-scumming a lockpick is not a mechanic worth protecting.

**The quest log records, it does not direct.** Entries say what you were told
and who told you. No map marker. One optional "last known location" hint per
quest, and you get it by asking someone in the world.

**Difficulty changes damage, never placement.** Three settings, ±25% on damage
dealt and taken. Spawn placement and loot are identical at every setting,
because placement is the game.

**The moon is a light source.** Night in the original is dark to the point of
being unnavigable without a torch. Ours keeps the darkness and adds a dim, cold,
directional moon — enough to read a silhouette and a road, not enough to fight
by. A player should want a torch, not need one to find the gate.
